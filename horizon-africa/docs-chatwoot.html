What is Chatwoot?
Chatwoot is a customer support platform that connects to your WhatsApp Business number. When the AI assistant ("Layla") encounters a query it cannot handle — such as fibre availability checks, discount requests, or requests to speak to a human — the conversation is automatically handed over to Chatwoot. You can then continue the conversation directly with the customer in real time.

3. Finding AI-Handed-Over Chats

When the AI assistant escalates a conversation to a human agent, it automatically creates a new conversation in Chatwoot. Here's how to find them:

Step 1: Go to Conversations

Click the Conversations icon (chat bubble) in the left sidebar. This is usually the default view when you log in.

Step 2: Check the "Open" Tab

Make sure you're on the Open tab. All newly handed-over conversations appear here. They will have a blue unread badge showing the number of unread messages.

Step 3: Use Filters

Use the filter pills to narrow down conversations:

  • All — Show all open conversations
  • Mine — Show only conversations assigned to you
  • Unassigned — Show conversations that haven't been assigned to anyone yet
How to know it's an AI handover: Conversations handed over by the AI will appear as new WhatsApp conversations. The AI also adds a private note to the conversation with a summary of what was discussed (see section 5).

4. Using the Chat Feature

Once you've found a conversation, click on it to open the chat window. You can now respond to the customer directly via WhatsApp.

Sending a Message

  1. Click the message input box at the bottom of the chat window
  2. Type your message
  3. Press Enter or click the Send button

Message Types

  • Customer messages appear on the left in white bubbles
  • Your messages appear on the right in blue bubbles

Using Private Notes

Click the Notes tab (next to the message input) to switch to private notes. Notes are internal comments visible only to your team — the customer cannot see them. Use notes to:

  • Leave context for other team members
  • Record what action was taken
  • Flag important information about the customer

5. Understanding Handover Notes

When the AI escalates a conversation, it automatically creates a private note with a summary of the conversation so far. This note typically includes:

  • Customer name and phone number
  • Product interest (e.g. Fibre, 5G)
  • Recommended package from the AI conversation
  • Email and physical address (if provided by the customer)
  • Reason for escalation (e.g. "Customer asked about fibre availability")
  • Conversation summary — a brief description of what was discussed
Why read the handover note first? The note gives you full context of what the AI already discussed with the customer, so you don't ask them to repeat information. This makes the handover seamless and professional.

Common Escalation Reasons

The AI escalates to Chatwoot when a customer:

  • Asks about fibre availability at a specific address
  • Requests a discount or better pricing
  • Asks for a callback from a human consultant
  • Requests to speak to a human agent
  • Has a complex query the AI cannot resolve

6. Resolving Conversations

Once you've finished helping a customer, mark the conversation as Resolved to remove it from the Open list and keep your workspace clean.

How to Resolve

  1. Open the conversation you want to resolve
  2. Click the Resolve button (checkmark icon) in the top-right of the chat header
  3. The conversation moves to the Resolved tab

7. Tips & Best Practices

Do's

  • Read the handover note first before responding to the customer
  • Respond promptly — the customer is waiting for a human reply
  • Use the customer's name — the AI already collected it
  • Reference what was discussed — e.g. "I see you're interested in our Premium Fibre package..."
  • Resolve conversations when the issue is handled
  • Use private notes to keep your team informed

Don'ts

  • Don't ask the customer to repeat information the AI already collected
  • Don't leave conversations unresolved if the issue is handled
  • Don't send private notes as regular messages — the customer can see those
  • Don't ignore unread badges — customers are waiting for a response
